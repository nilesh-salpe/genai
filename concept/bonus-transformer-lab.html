Good to know

  • Loss bounces around step to step — that's normal. Each step trains on one random batch sampled from the corpus, not the whole thing, so loss is noisy even while trending down over many steps.
  • Matrix colors are relative to that matrix, not absolute. Switching between weight matrices rescales the color range to each one's own max magnitude — a "bright" cell in a small matrix isn't necessarily a large number in absolute terms.
  • Changing architecture resets training from scratch. Adjusting embedding dim, heads, blocks, FFN hidden, or activation rebuilds the model with fresh random weights — it doesn't adapt the model you were just watching train.
  • Loss and P(correct) are the same underlying number. Loss is literally −log(P(correct)) — as the blue line (probability) rises toward 1, the amber line (loss) falls toward 0. Watching them diverge would mean something's actually broken.
  • A random, untrained model would score loss ≈ — (that's ln(vocab size) — uniform probability over every character). Everything below that is the model doing better than guessing.
