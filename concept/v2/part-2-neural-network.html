The box from Part 0, made of real numbers

Part 0's "dials that get nudged toward less wrong" now gets an actual formula for each piece: why the dials need a kink between them, how to turn raw scores into honest probabilities, how to measure "wrong" as one number, and exactly which direction to nudge every dial. By the end, "train a network" stops being a metaphor.

01Why stacking needs a kink

Without a bend, depth is an illusion

Stack two linear layers and expand the algebra: W₂(W₁x+b₁)+b₂ = (W₂W₁)x + (W₂b₁+b₂) — one matrix, one bias, dressed up as two layers. A thousand linear layers still collapse to one. A linear layer can only stretch, rotate, and shift space; do that any number of times and straight lines stay straight — but the boundary between "spam" and "not spam" is rarely a straight line.

The fix is a kink between layers — the standard choice is ReLU: ReLU(x) = max(0, x), applied to every number independently. One bend at the origin per unit, but bend enough of them and the network traces any curve you like.

02Scores into beliefs

Softmax: any list of numbers becomes a probability distribution

A network's last layer outputs logits — raw, unbounded numbers, one per class, meaningless alone. Softmax turns them into a proper probability distribution:

softmax(z)ᵢ = eᶻⁱ ⁄ Σⱼ eᶻʲ

Exponentiating first guarantees every output is positive (no negative probabilities); dividing by the sum guarantees they add to 1. Because eᵃ/eᵇ = eᵃ⁻ᵇ, only the differences between logits matter — add 100 to every logit and softmax doesn't move at all.

03Beliefs into blame

Cross-entropy: scoring how wrong a belief was

Assign probability p to the outcome that actually happens. How wrong were you to have doubted it? Score it as surprise: zero surprise for certainty that turned out right, unbounded surprise for confident certainty that turned out wrong. −log p does exactly this — −log(1) = 0, and −log(p) → ∞ as p → 0.

L = −log P(correct class)

Averaged surprise against the true distribution is entropy; averaged surprise using a model's distribution is cross-entropy — and cross-entropy minus entropy is KL divergence, the extra surprise paid specifically for the model being wrong. Since entropy of the (fixed) training data never changes, minimizing cross-entropy and minimizing KL divergence are the same act: pushing the model's beliefs to match reality as closely as possible.

This is also maximum likelihood. The probability the model assigns to the entire training set is a product of many small numbers; taking logs turns that product into a sum, and maximizing Σ log P(correct) is the same as minimizing −Σ log P(correct) — cross-entropy. "Train a language model" and "find parameters that make the observed text most likely" are the same sentence.

04Learning

Gradient descent: the nudge, made precise

Part 1 built the gradient — the direction of steepest increase. Training wants the loss to go down, so move every parameter a small step the opposite way:

w ← w − η ∂L/∂w

η (eta), the learning rate, is the step size. Too small and training crawls; too large and each step overshoots the minimum and the loss climbs instead of falling.

05By hand

Backpropagation on a network small enough to check

Two inputs → one hidden unit (ReLU) → one output, no biases, to keep every number checkable by hand. Forward pass: h = ReLU(w₁x₁ + w₂x₂), ŷ = w₃h. With w₁=0.5, w₂=−0.3, w₃=0.8, input x=[2,1], target y=1:

h = ReLU(0.5×2 + (−0.3)×1) = ReLU(0.7) = 0.7    ŷ = 0.8×0.7 = 0.56    L = (ŷ−y)² = (0.56−1)² = 0.1936

Backward pass — the chain rule, applied three times. ∂L/∂ŷ = 2(ŷ−y) = −0.88. Then ∂L/∂w₃ = ∂L/∂ŷ · h = −0.88×0.7 = −0.616. And ∂L/∂h = ∂L/∂ŷ · w₃ = −0.88×0.8 = −0.704, which (since h>0, ReLU's derivative is 1) passes straight through: ∂L/∂w₁ = ∂L/∂h · x₁ = −0.704×2 = −1.408, ∂L/∂w₂ = ∂L/∂h · x₂ = −0.704×1 = −0.704.

Every one of those is the chain rule from Part 1, applied once per edge in the computation, working backward. A billion-parameter model runs the identical procedure — more edges, same rule, every time.

06What goes wrong

Vanishing gradients, and the fix

Deep stacks multiply many local derivatives together via the chain rule. Sigmoid's derivative never exceeds 0.25 — stack ten sigmoid layers and the gradient shrinks by up to 0.25¹⁰ ≈ 10⁻⁶ before it reaches the first layer, which then barely updates at all. ReLU's derivative is exactly 1 on the positive side, so signal passes through undamaged — and a residual connection, x + F(x), adds a guaranteed +1 to the derivative regardless of what F does, a bypass road the gradient can always travel however deep the stack.

A second failure, at the start rather than during training: initialize every weight in a layer to zero, and every neuron computes exactly the same thing forever — identical outputs mean identical gradients mean identical updates. Random initialization breaks that symmetry; the scale of the randomness is chosen so activations neither explode nor vanish as they pass through each layer.

What Part 3 assumes from here
