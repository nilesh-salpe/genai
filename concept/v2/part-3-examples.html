The same machine, four problems

Nothing new gets built on this page — every formula is from Part 2. What changes is the problem: a decision boundary that isn't a line, an image instead of a table of numbers, and two flavors of "which of these matters right now" that quietly rehearse Part 4's attention before it has a name.

01Not a line

A spiral, and why one straight cut can't solve it

Two classes of points, arranged as interleaved spirals — no straight line separates them, no matter how it's angled. A network with a kink (Part 2 §1) can bend its decision boundary to match; a purely linear one is stuck guessing.

02Not a bag of pixels

Why images want a filter, not a flatten

Flatten an image into one long vector and a dense layer treats every pixel as unrelated to its neighbors — position (0,0) and position (1,0) are adjacent in the image but arbitrary strangers in the vector. A convolution keeps the 2D grid: one small filter slides over every position, computing the same dot product each time, so "detect an edge" is learned once and applies everywhere.

03Which one matters

Retrieval by similarity: a preview of attention

Three stored (key, value) pairs and one query. Score the query against every key with a dot product, softmax the scores into weights, then return the weighted average of the values — not the single best match, a blend weighted by how well each key matched.

04Not allowed to peek

Predicting the next character, without seeing the future

Train a tiny model to predict the next character of "ababab..." from the characters before it. At training time, every position's prediction must depend only on positions before it — otherwise the "prediction" is just copying an answer it was handed. A causal mask enforces this: position i is blocked from attending to any position j > i.

05Onward

What Part 4 formalizes

The spiral and the filter were Part 2's machinery on new shapes of data. The retrieval example and the causal mask were rehearsals — the exact arithmetic and the exact rule Part 4 is about to name properly, derive from scratch, and assemble into the mechanism every current LLM runs.
