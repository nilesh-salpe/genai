The full architecture

Attention alone isn't a network — it needs to be wrapped, stacked, and (in the paper's original design) split into two cooperating stacks. This page assembles the block, explains why translation wanted two stacks talking to each other, and covers the last mile from a trained model's raw output to an actual chosen word.

01Assembling the block

Attention wrapped in residuals and normalization

One Transformer block is attention (Part 4) and a small feed-forward network (two linear layers with a kink between them, exactly Part 2's machine), each wrapped in a residual connection and a layer normalization:

x ← x + Attention(LayerNorm(x))    x ← x + FFN(LayerNorm(x))

The residual's +x is Part 2 §6's fix for vanishing gradients — a guaranteed path back to every earlier layer, however deep the stack. LayerNorm rescales each token's vector to have a stable mean and spread before every sub-layer sees it, so training doesn't have to fight ever-drifting activation scales on top of everything else.

02Two stacks, two jobs

Why the original paper used an encoder and a decoder

Translating "the cat sat" into French: to correctly place the feminine adjective ending, the model needs the whole source sentence available at once, with no restriction on looking ahead — there's no "future" in a sentence that's already fully given. But generating the French output must be causal — token 5 can't be produced by peeking at the token-6 answer. One stack can't satisfy both needs at once, so the paper used two: an encoder (full attention, no mask, reads the source once) and a decoder (causal self-attention, generates the target one token at a time).

StackSelf-attentionRuns
EncoderFull — every position sees every positionOnce per input sentence
DecoderCausal — masked, no looking ahead (Part 4 §4)Once per generated token

Cross-attention: the wire between them

The decoder needs to consult the encoder's finished understanding of the source. Cross-attention is the identical formula from Part 4 §1 — only the source of Q, K, V changes: Q comes from the decoder's own progress so far, K and V come from the encoder's output. No mask is needed here — the encoder's output is a fully finished, already-complete thing, nothing about it is "in the future."

03From numbers to a word

Sampling: turning a distribution into a choice

The stack's final layer produces logits over the vocabulary; softmax turns them into a probability distribution (Part 2 §2). Generation still needs to pick one token from that distribution — always the top pick and text gets repetitive; too random and it stops making sense. Three knobs, all reshaping the same distribution before a token is drawn:

KnobWhat it does
TemperatureDivides logits before softmax — below 1 sharpens toward the top pick, above 1 flattens toward uniform (Part 2 §2)
Top-kZero out every probability outside the k largest, then renormalize and sample
Top-p (nucleus)Keep the smallest set of top candidates whose probabilities sum to p, then sample only from those
