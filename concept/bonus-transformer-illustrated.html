Common misconceptions

    
    
      
  • Layer, head, and neuron are three different, nested things. A layer is a full transformation stage (this page stacks 4); inside its attention step it has several heads; inside its FFN it has several neurons. A head is never called a neuron, and neither is a layer.
    • 
      
  • Multi-head attention isn't "split one projection into pieces." We do that here only to keep every number traceable by hand — real models give each head its own independent, full-size W_Q/W_K/W_V, learned separately.
    • 
      
  • Attention itself has no sense of order. It just weights and sums vectors — position only enters the model once, at the positional-encoding step, before attention ever runs.
    • 
      
  • Causal masking is a decoder thing. It exists because a decoder predicts the next token and must not see the future. Encoder-only models (like BERT) deliberately skip it and let every position see the whole sequence.
    • 
      
  • The √d_k in attention isn't about the number of heads. It counteracts dot products growing large in high dimensions, which would otherwise push softmax toward a near one-hot output and stall learning almost everywhere else.
    • 
      
  • Fine-tuning isn't a different algorithm. It's the exact same forward → loss → backward → update loop as training — the only differences are the starting weights (pretrained, not random) and usually a smaller learning rate.
    • 
      
  • This walkthrough uses one training example for clarity. Real training batches many examples together and averages their loss before every single weight update.
    •
